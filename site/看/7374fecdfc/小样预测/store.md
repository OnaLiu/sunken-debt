## Features

- **Every haul is a mystery box.** Everything you salvage comes up caked in thick mud, old net and shell. Hose it off to find out what it is — the moment the shape is clear, the rest of the crust bursts away.
- **From one net to a whole hold.** Start by picking a spot and dropping the net yourself, one reveal at a time. Later you stack a full deck of mud bundles and blast them open together, and finally open an entire cargo hold at once — the most valuable find always comes last.
- **Hand it over to your crew.** Hire a crane operator who drops the net for you, a navigator who sails the routes you know, then buy more boats. Your fleet keeps salvaging even while the game is closed.
- **Upgrades you can see.** A wider net, a longer crane arm, a bigger fuel tank, heavier net teeth, pumps and hoses, lights and sonar — every upgrade is fitted onto the boat and changes what you can reach and how much you haul.
- **A sea that gets deeper and stranger.** Four seas, from a clear shallow cove down to where no sunlight reaches. The deeper you go, the darker the water, the more valuable the haul — and the less right it feels.
- **A debt you can actually pay off.** Clear the debt page by page; each page opens a deeper sea. No interest, no deadlines. Paying the last page is the ending, about 3–5 hours in.
- Real wrecks, real seabed: what you see on the seabed is what comes up in the net. Crabs and fish live down there.
- Keyboard & mouse and controller.

## Short description

You inherited a boat and a debt you can't pay. Drop nets on real wrecks, hose the mud off every haul to see what you've found, upgrade your boat, hire a crew and a fleet — and dive into ever deeper, stranger seas until the debt is paid.

## About this game

The boat came with a debt — a drowned captain's debt, and it is owed to the sea.

You start with one boat, one crane and a shallow cove: Ebbtide Cove, where a medieval merchant ship lies on its side in the sand. Sail out, find the wreck, pick a spot and drop the net. Everything comes up caked in mud. Your crew hoses it down and the crust cracks away piece by piece — a copper pot, a sealed sack, a coin chest full of silver.

Every trip lasts as long as your fuel and your hold. Bring the haul back to the dock, fit new gear onto your boat, and head out on the next tide — each tide washes new cargo out of the wreck, and each trip brings back more than the last.

Soon one net at a time isn't enough. Widen the deck and stack the mud bundles, then wash the whole deck open at once, cheapest first and the best find last. Hire a crane operator to work the nets, a navigator to sail the routes you know, and buy more boats until a whole fleet goes out with the tide.

Then go deeper. A narrow strait full of wrecks from every age, every bow pointing to shore. A lightless layer where only your own lamps show anything. And at the very bottom, the last page of the debt.

Mostly, you'll understand what you see down there. Sometimes you won't.
